# Product Constitution

1. The platform must sell itself first through the Internal Company Workspace.
2. Companies buy outcomes, not collections of AI buttons.
3. Trust, privacy, and deliverability outrank maximum automation.
4. No evidence means no verified fact; inference is labeled.
5. Every important internal and external action is explainable and auditable.
6. Every autonomous action has tenant, budget, volume, retry, timeout, approval, and stop boundaries.
7. Customer workspaces, memory, messages, engagement, and strategy never mix.
8. Platform-wide collision controls expose no tenant identity or competitive intelligence.
9. External outreach requires current immutable approval, suppression checks, and a configured safe delivery mode.
10. Humans retain responsibility for strategy, pricing, negotiation, proposals, contracts, commitments, and closing.
11. Test, estimated, projected, and observed production data are never presented as equivalent.
12. Failures and unavailable providers are visible; inactive features are not presented as working.
13. The interface should feel like managing a workforce, not administering database tables.
14. Customer data belongs to the customer and is retained only within configured policy boundaries.
15. Professional legal review remains necessary; the product assists policy enforcement but does not promise universal compliance.
